Each task resamples real observations and repeats the period search. A serial baseline runs the same tasks first; a process pool then distributes them across workers. The result is a resampling distribution and a measured scaling experiment.

Local workers are processes on this computer, not separate physical cluster nodes. Timings include orchestration and process startup. Speedup can be below 1×.

A SMALL OBSERVATORY. A BIG QUESTION.

What can a changing star tell us?

Mira variables are pulsating, evolved giants with long brightness cycles. THOTH brings published catalogue records and real photometry together so you can inspect those cycles, search for periods, and compare an empirical model with the observations.

About the catalogues and the math +

Catalogue records retain their published classifications and identifiers. OGLE regions and GCVS records can overlap; the record count is not a count of unique stars or a complete census of every known Mira. Missing values remain missing. Positions are in degrees; periods are in days; I and V refer to photometric bands.

The native C++ engine fits a weighted Fourier series over candidate frequencies and returns the best period within your search interval. Python serves the catalogue and coordinates the analysis. The displayed curve describes the observed variability; it is not a physical stellar evolution or pulsation simulation. Sparse sampling, aliases, changing periods, bandpass, and underestimated errors can all affect a fit.

Photometry availability varies by record. The bundled demonstration uses a real OGLE Mira light curve; source links lead to the published data. THOTH does not infer distances, temperatures, masses, or radii from a catalogue magnitude.
